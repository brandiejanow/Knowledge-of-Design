An introduction

What is Knowledge of Design?

A global initiative dedicated to advancing design as a field of knowledge.

Grounded in the STEAM fields, it examines how design informs systems, environments, decisions, and how we live. Its activity spans research, publications, workshops, partnerships, and an annual gathering that draws together perspectives from across sectors and areas of expertise.

Since its founding, KoD has developed an intellectual framework, a methodology, a body of knowledge, a research agenda, an archive, and a community of contributors. Together, these position design not only as a profession but as a lens through which relationships, behaviour, culture, and the conditions that influence daily life can be explored.

This activity continues throughout the year through collaborations, educational initiatives, research, and public programming.

Annual theme structure
Each year, a distinct theme frames the central question being explored, guiding session selection, dialogue, and intellectual direction. The theme serves as a research lens as much as a program framework.

Why it exists

Across fields, not inside silos.

KoD was founded in response to a growing disconnect between fields that influence one another. Specialists across science, technology, engineering, artistic practice, business, research, and culture often investigate similar challenges without a common place to exchange ideas. Valuable expertise existed throughout these areas, yet few spaces brought them together.

At the same time, few areas of practice influence the world as much as design. It informs products, environments, infrastructure, services, organizations, and daily experiences. Despite this reach, it is often viewed through a narrow professional lens rather than as a source of insight that can inform broader conversations.

The platform was created to connect areas of expertise, encourage new lines of inquiry, and support greater understanding between communities that rarely engage directly. Grounded in the STEAM disciplines, it offers a framework for examining shared challenges, emerging opportunities, and the role design plays in life.

“Humans make up only 0.01 percent of all life on Earth by biomass. However, human activity has reshaped nearly every natural system. Over 80 percent of wild mammals and approximately 50 percent of plant life have disappeared due to human impact.

Design and architecture influence this story. Every material, structure, and system we create affects how life survives. Life-centric design repositions life, rather than humans, as the measure of success. It integrates science, technology, engineering, art, and mathematics to design living systems rather than over them. The goal is regeneration and coexistence, not extraction.

This is why KoD exists: to ask what design owes to life, and to bring together architects, engineers, artists, and scientists who see design as a force for repair and renewal.”

Brandie Janow · Founder, Knowledge of Design

A way of thinking

Design as inquiry

KoD is grounded in speculative design: the practice of using design to ask what futures are possible, and what kind of world our choices are building toward.

Beyond asking how to improve something, speculative design asks “what if?” It builds scenarios, artifacts, and provocations that make possible futures tangible enough to question and debate now, while there is still time to choose between them. Design becomes a tool for foresight and inquiry.

Design as a question

An idea, a prototype, or a provocation that opens inquiry instead of a finished object that closes it.

Futures, plural

There is no single inevitable future. Speculative design holds several possible ones up to the light so they can be examined and questioned.

Knowledge over output

The value lies in what we learn and document along the way, as much as in what gets built at the end.

A shared method

It belongs to scientists, engineers, and artists alike, which is why it sits at the centre of a STEAM platform.

The STEAM framework

Five fields, one system.

These five fields are treated as an integrated system, not separate disciplines. Design operates across all of them, connecting, translating, and converting knowledge into usable systems. It is not the outcome. It is the process.
