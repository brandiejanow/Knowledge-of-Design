Designing at a national scale · SXSW London 2026.

First global edition · SXSW

London 2026

83 Rivington Street, Arch 3 · June 3, 2026

As KSA moves through rapid cultural, urban, and economic transformation, design is playing a growing role in how people move, gather, learn, participate, and experience public life.

This session brought together voices across architecture, urbanism, museums, research, cultural infrastructure, and interdisciplinary practice to examine how design can guide transformation on a national scale. The conversation explored the influence of environments on behavior, identity, perception, storytelling, material culture, public experience, and emerging industries.

The panel moved across creative infrastructure, design knowledge, urban experience, and future growth, asking how design can support systems built around people, places, and everyday life.

Knowledge of Design Research

Making a Design Culture

A critical dialogue on agency, experimentation, and material capacity.

Lead Researcher and Author: Brandie Janow
Based on a critical dialogue with Harry Dobbs and Paulo Pimentel.

Drawn from the exchanges behind the SXSW London session, this paper examines how a design culture is built through the interplay of identity, industry, and sustained cultural development.

The necessity of making

Design cannot thrive in a vacuum. Physical making, through 3D printing, batch production, and local manufacturing, is essential to design quality and a sustainable industry.

Against homogenisation

The risk of every designer producing the same motifs. A nascent industry needs diverse innovation, not identical products, to find its authentic voice.

A safe space to fail

Designers need room to sketch, experiment, take risks, and make mistakes. The iterative, messy process is how a design culture matures.

Provenance over import

Moving beyond imported best practice toward innovations that arise where culture, technology, and place meet.
