Design
Ecosystems

The study of design ecosystems extends beyond cataloguing institutions, practitioners and programs to examine the relationships shaping capability, participation and continuity.

How do people, resources, institutions and knowledge interact to support original practice over time? This inquiry examines the connections through which a design culture develops, adapts and sustains its work.

01 / The question

Activity is not the same as an ecosystem.

An ecosystem is often described as a network of interconnected people, organizations, resources and systems. That description identifies its components, but tells us little about how well they work together.

A city can have visible festivals, new institutions and growing investment while practitioners still lack places to experiment, ways to produce, routes to markets or the ability to shape decisions. The existence of activity does not establish that relationships are functioning or that growth can continue.

Rather than treating the word ecosystem as a conclusion, this inquiry uses it as a research question: what supports participation, exchange, original practice and continuity?

02 / What belongs in the system?

Map the conditions, not just the names.

The following areas provide a working map. No single place needs to organize them in the same way, and the categories overlap.

01

People and practice

Designers, makers, researchers, creative businesses, clients, audiences and informal communities.

02

Education and knowledge

Universities, training, mentorship, research, publications, critique and access to expertise.

03

Institutions and governance

Professional bodies, cultural institutions, public policy, regulation and decision-making authority.

04

Production and infrastructure

Workshops, materials, tools, digital fabrication, suppliers, technical skills and facilities.

05

Economy and opportunity

Funding, commissions, procurement, market access, professional pathways and demand.

06

Public life and continuity

Exhibitions, media, archives, libraries, museums, public feedback and institutions that preserve learning.

03 / Relationships and gaps

The connections determine what is possible.

An inventory reveals who and what exists. Ecosystem research also has to examine access, influence, exchange, dependencies and the spaces between actors. This includes asking where decisions originate and whether knowledge and capability remain after a project ends.

Talent without pathways

Skills may exist without mentoring, paid work, commissioning or routes into practice.

Funding without participation

Investment can produce activity without strengthening local authorship or shared capability.

Visibility without depth

Events and promotion may grow faster than critique, research, production or public understanding.

Infrastructure without continuity

Facilities and programs can disappear from practice when maintenance, stewardship and knowledge transfer are missing.

These are diagnostic possibilities, not claims that any particular geography displays them. Each must be tested against the evidence available for the place being studied.

04 / Design culture

Five connected conditions

The published Knowledge of Design research Making a Design Culture identifies five interdependent conditions. They offer a complementary lens for studying how ecosystem structures influence practice and public life.

  1. AgencyWho can frame questions, exercise judgment and influence direction?
  2. Original practiceWhat supports inquiry and work grounded in a distinct point of view?
  3. ExperimentationCan people test, critique, revise and learn before an outcome is fixed?
  4. Material capacityDo the skills, materials and production pathways exist to bring ideas into use?
  5. Public life over timeCan work be encountered, evaluated, sustained and developed across generations?

05 / Change over time

An ecosystem is not a snapshot.

Institutions form, responsibilities change, skills circulate, policies shift and initiatives sometimes stop. Studying a single year can conceal those movements. Longitudinal research can distinguish announcements from operation, growth from reliance on one actor, and momentum from lasting capacity.

Questions to return to each year

  • What became possible that was not possible before?
  • Which activities continued, expanded, changed direction or stalled?
  • Where did relationships strengthen, thin out or disappear?
  • Who gained influence, access to resources or the ability to produce?
  • What knowledge was documented, transferred or lost?

06 / Research method

Build the framework from evidence.

This working approach combines observation, research conversations, documentary sources, sector and institutional records, practice-based inquiry, comparative study and futures methods. The research must distinguish a stated ambition from an operational system and a personal account from a representative finding.

Observe

Identify actors, pathways, resources, access, participation and recurring activity.

Connect

Examine dependencies and relationships rather than treating entries as isolated data points.

Compare

Trace what changes across time, locations and different research sources.

Question

Use foresight to explore emerging tensions and possibilities without presenting scenarios as predictions.

This framework is organized as a research agenda. Conceptual categories, published arguments and questions for future investigation are distinguished in the accompanying research record.
