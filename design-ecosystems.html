Knowledge of Design / Research development / 17 to 18 October 2026

Design Ecosystems

Investigating the people, decisions, materials and living systems involved in design.

This inquiry began with a close examination of who contributes to design, who shapes its direction and how its consequences extend across a project's life. A recorded scoping discussion, museum field documentation and a set of professional and academic benchmarks inform the research questions now being developed.

Research by Brandie JanowStudy status: scoping and comparative analysis

02 / Scoping conversation

From project roles to the wider system

The 18 October recorded scoping discussion (8 minutes, 33 seconds) developed a broader view of the contributors and conditions surrounding design. The working analysis examines four connected dimensions.

01

Contribution

Designers, makers, specialist producers, researchers, workers and the expertise that shapes the work.

02

Decision-making

Who writes a brief, sets requirements, authorizes changes, and has influence throughout delivery.

03

Recognition

How work is credited, how responsibility is distributed and how expertise enters public records.

04

Consequences

What happens across production, use, repair and after-use for people, materials, habitats and other living systems.

03 / Museum field documentation

What sixteen observations contribute

Photographs shared on 18 October capture exhibition material on designer, maker and user relationships, ethical manufacturing, material cycles, credited production, public research and design education. The working analysis organizes them into six clusters.

01 / Agency

Design briefs move between roles.

The museum's Designer Maker User materials show makers as active contributors to specification and users as participants in the life of designed objects.

02 / Production

Materials carry process histories.

Textile lifecycle displays and fabrication documentation reveal successive acts of preparation, processing, finishing, repair and recovery.

03 / Knowledge

Public institutions make research visible.

Research residencies, educational briefs and exhibition credits make knowledge production and specialist contributions available for study.

04 / Life-centric inquiry

Expanding who design accounts for

The October research position considers people involved in production and people affected through use, alongside animals, habitats, material systems and ecological change. It asks how these relationships can be studied in the same project.

01SourceMaterial origins, ecology and extraction
02MakeWorker expertise, conditions and decisions
03UseUsers, communities, care and maintenance
04ContinueRepair, recovery, waste and habitat effects

The sequence is a working research lens connecting material circularity to labour and ecological responsibility. It draws on the October scoping position and the field documentation of textile lifecycles.

05 / Research programme

Questions prepared for empirical testing

Eight proposals in the benchmark workbook focus directly on ecosystem relationships. They identify the evidence needed to develop the research beyond its initial scoping phase.

T-01

Across disciplines

Compare role maps in architecture, product or textile design, service design and public programming.

T-02

Credit and recognition

Study project rosters, contracts and published acknowledgements to trace the work contributors perform.

T-03

Community influence

Compare participatory contributions with revisions to briefs, drawings and final decisions.

T-04

Working conditions

Develop a safe, consent-led approach to labour experience with specialist research guidance.

T-05 to T-07

Materials, habitats and outcomes

Develop linked studies of traceability, multi-species effects and the distribution of project consequences.

T-08

Museum recognition

Compare public credits, project records and documented contributions across exhibitions and collections.
