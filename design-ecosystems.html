Knowledge of Design / Ongoing research programme

Design Ecosystems

Understanding the relationships that shape design, from the people who make decisions and produce work to the environments and living systems affected by it.

This research develops a layered account of design ecosystems. It brings together professional roles, knowledge exchange, institutional influence, material processes, cultural participation and the long-term consequences of design. The purpose is to examine how these relationships operate together and where further investigation can deepen our understanding.

Research and analysis by Brandie Janow

Research model

The layers of a design ecosystem

Our working model brings the people involved in a project into conversation with the wider conditions shaping their work. Developed through recent research discussions, including contributions from Nathan, the layers represent connected perspectives that can be studied together.

Research questions

How these relationships work

At the center of the inquiry are the people whose knowledge, labor, decisions and lived experience influence design. The emerging questions address authorship, influence, participation and the responsibilities that continue throughout a project's life.

Who shapes the direction?

How are questions framed, briefs developed and decisions negotiated among commissioners, practitioners and communities?

Who contributes knowledge?

How do research, technical skill, making, lived experience and local understanding inform the process?

How is expertise recognized?

How are contributions documented, credited and understood across collaborative work and production?

Where do consequences travel?

How do choices affect workers, users, communities, materials, habitats and future conditions?

Primary field documentation

Learning from design practice

Field documentation captured at the Design Museum in London in May 2025 contributed observations on the relationships between designers, makers and users, the visibility of production expertise, ethical manufacturing and the lifecycle of materials.

People and authorship

Design is collectively shaped.

Exhibition material foregrounded the role of makers, collaborators, producers and users alongside the people credited with directing a project.

Production and materiality

Making carries its own knowledge.

Fabrication records, textile processes and material displays revealed the decisions, skills and technical relationships involved in bringing work into use.

Public knowledge

Institutions preserve the process.

Research residencies, collection interpretation and educational programmes showed how design knowledge is shared, questioned and carried forward.

Primary visual field documentation: Design Museum, London, May 2025. Observations have been organized within the ongoing Knowledge of Design research archive.

Life-centric research

Design across its whole life

The layered model brings material origins, labor, use, maintenance and ecological effects into one research perspective. It creates a basis for examining responsibility across time and across the people and living systems connected to a design.

Continuing inquiry

Where the research is developing

The next phase brings the layered model into project documentation and comparative studies. It will examine how responsibilities are distributed, how communities influence outcomes, and how contributions are represented in records of design work.

Map participation

Compare contributors and decision pathways across different types of design practice.

Trace authorship

Examine project credits, production records and the roles through which expertise becomes visible.

Follow influence

Study how research, engagement and feedback affect briefs and subsequent decisions.

Study consequences

Connect material lifecycles, work, use and ecological effects within selected cases.

Brandie Janow also curates Design Reservoir, a global design research directory, and publishes Saudi Creative Economy, her independent study of the Saudi creative sector. Each project has its own research scope.
