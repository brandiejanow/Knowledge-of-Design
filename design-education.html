Design Education and Its Future

What should design education prepare people to do, and what support do graduates need when they enter professional practice?

Lead Researcher and Author: Brandie Janow   |   Foundational research: December 2024   |   KoD study: 2026 onward

Design education reaches beyond creative and technical skills. Students encounter questions about research, responsible decisions, business agreements, production, cultural understanding, technological change, and how to enter professional work. This study examines what belongs in a contemporary education, what needs practical experience, and how universities and employers can better connect learning with professional life.

01 / Background

Where the research began

In December 2024, a detailed set of independently written recommendations was prepared for an Industrial Design Advisory Board discussion at Iowa State University. The questions addressed professional preparation, research, emerging technology, STEAM, international practice, ethics, making, and relationships between education and industry.

The 2024 work provides a dated starting point for a wider KoD study. It is not presented as a university policy document or evidence that any institution adopted its recommendations. The institutional advisory role and the independent authorship of the work remain distinct.

03 / Research model

From education to practice

A student's professional preparation is not limited to what happens in the classroom. The 2024 recommendations connect research and judgment with technical work, outside relationships, and what happens after graduation. This diagram groups those connections into five areas for further study.

Learning

Design history, STEAM, cultural knowledge, research, and principles used to evaluate decisions.

Testing

Prototypes, field research, critique, experimentation, and the consequences of design choices.

Professional skills

Contracts, intellectual property, scope, communication, business, and responsibility for delivery.

Industry access

Studios, employers, technical experts, manufacturing, mentors, and collaborative projects.

Continuity

Early career support, professional networks, continued learning, and evidence of outcomes.

Conceptual grouping derived from the topics in the 2024 recommendations. The sequence is a research model, not a measured pathway or a claim that each stage is supplied by every institution.

04 / Continuing questions

What needs to be examined next

The next stage is to test these questions using documented educational programs and the experiences of students, graduates, faculty, and practitioners. It is not enough to count new subjects in a curriculum. The research needs to examine what learners are able to do with them.

Preparation

Which professional responsibilities do students understand before their first paid project, and which are learned only after graduation?

Access

Who can obtain mentors, studios, equipment, professional contacts, and relevant work experience?

Interdisciplinary work

How do design students learn to work with science, engineering, technology, humanities, cultural practice, and public services?

Results

What evidence would show that a course, project, placement, or institutional partnership has been useful to the people involved?
