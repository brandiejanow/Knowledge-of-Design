Advisory Board research

Design industry insights

Documentation gap

Research conversations highlighted documentation of design processes, decisions and reasoning as important to knowledge transfer and cumulative learning.

AI and meaning

AI is generating outputs that bypass the thinking process. The danger lies less in the tool and more in designers ceasing to ask why.

Saudi culture as material

Untapped potential in cultural heritage, from woodwork and craft to poetry and narrative, as source material for contemporary design.

Financial literacy

Designers struggle to quantify the value they create. The financial aspect is identified as the biggest structural weakness in the industry, everywhere in the world.

Speed vs depth

The development timeline moves at a pace that makes deep research and documentation difficult.

The charity perception

Research conversations raised questions about how local design is valued, including the relationships between community support, desirability, quality and commercial recognition.

From pattern to perspective

Open research questions

Meaning vs the sake of it

A recurring concern: many practitioners design for the sake of it. Meaning can come from inspiration and narrative, but also from responding rigorously to existing factors and constraints.

The teachable unknown

Aesthetics and composition combine learned principles, practice and personal judgment. The research asks how these skills can be taught through observation, critique and repeated experimentation.

Born or taught

The board landed on both. Skill, technique, and life experience move a designer through stages; some are more receptive to creativity, others to analysis. Mastery is a path that unfolds over time.

Value engineering reality

A perfect design meets the reality of budget and time. The skill is protecting value, and being strong enough to influence the client, when constraints push back.

Current research directions / October 2026

Questions shaping the next inquiry

These themes originated in Knowledge of Design advisory and public-programming exchanges. They have been reconsidered alongside recent research on participation, emerging technology, cultural knowledge, sensory experience and futures. Each opens a direction for investigation.

Participation and shared authorship

Research participants increasingly have roles in framing questions, interpreting experience and developing prototypes. The inquiry moves beyond collecting responses to examine who participates in decisions, how contributions are acknowledged and how expertise is shared.

Participatory Research Through Design, International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction (2026) ↗

Human judgment in AI-assisted practice

AI now participates in early inquiry, iteration, conceptual exploration and communication. A current question is how experience, critical judgment, transparency and responsibility influence outcomes when designers work with computational systems.

Human-AI co-creation and design cognition, DRS2026 ↗

Sensory experience and inclusive environments

Sound, light, texture, spatial configuration and other sensory conditions affect how people navigate and experience environments. Research is making sensory accessibility and wellbeing more central to questions of design quality and participation.

Sensory stimuli in the built environment for autistic people, 2026 scoping review ↗

Play as a research method

Game mechanics, simulation and playful material experiments can help people explore uncertain situations, rehearse collective decisions and generate research knowledge. The question extends beyond designing games to understanding what can be learned through their creation and use.

Research through game design, DRS2026 ↗

Participatory futures literacy

Foresight is becoming part of public learning and institutional practice, including in cultural organizations. Different groups can examine assumptions about the future, imagine alternatives and consider their implications for choices made today.

Futures literacy in museums and municipalities, UNESCO (2026) ↗

Heritage as a source of future practice

Material traditions, craft processes, conservation and archival knowledge can inform contemporary responses to environmental and social change. Research can examine how inherited expertise contributes to making, restoration, regenerative production and design education.

European Partnership for Resilient Cultural Heritage (2026 to 2035) ↗

The conditions for experimentation

Original practice relies on time to test, opportunities for critique, material skills and environments where uncertain ideas can develop. This connects design education to the wider question of how a culture sustains inquiry and learning over time.

Making a Design Culture, original Knowledge of Design research (2026) →
