Advisory Board research

Design industry insights

Documentation gap

Designers are not documenting their process, decisions, or reasoning. Without this, knowledge cannot be shared, transferred, or built upon.

AI and meaning

AI is generating outputs that bypass the thinking process. The danger lies less in the tool and more in designers ceasing to ask why.

Saudi culture as material

Untapped potential in cultural heritage, from woodwork and craft to poetry and narrative, as source material for contemporary design.

Financial literacy

Designers struggle to quantify the value they create. The financial aspect is identified as the biggest structural weakness in the industry, everywhere in the world.

Speed vs depth

The development timeline moves at a pace that makes deep research and documentation difficult.

The charity perception

Consumers often view buying from local designers as an act of charity rather than a choice based on desire or product quality.

From pattern to perspective

Open research questions

Meaning vs the sake of it

A recurring concern: many practitioners design for the sake of it. Meaning can come from inspiration and narrative, but also from responding rigorously to existing factors and constraints.

The teachable unknown

There is no clear rule for a visually pleasing composition. Talent plays a role, but those without obvious natural talent also deserve a pathway. How to make this teachable remains an open research question.

Born or taught

The board landed on both. Skill, technique, and life experience move a designer through stages; some are more receptive to creativity, others to analysis. Mastery is a path that unfolds over time.

Value engineering reality

A perfect design meets the reality of budget and time. The skill is protecting value, and being strong enough to influence the client, when constraints push back.

Design Futures & Signals

Signals we are watching

These weak signals were gathered through advisory-board research and the exchanges behind the SXSW London session. None is presented as a certainty; each is preserved as a thread worth following.

JOMO: The Joy of Missing Out

Coined by Sal Kurdi-Serafi. A counter-movement to sensory overload. As experiences become saturated, design turns toward simplicity, smaller groups, and genuine human connection over mass appeal.

From buzzwords to real language

Practitioners are abandoning borrowed jargon like generic sustainability talk, and arriving with their own vocabulary, explaining what they actually mean. A quiet shift toward semantic honesty.

Research replaces focus groups

A move away from focus groups toward research groups and feedback loops, evidence and lived experience over assumption. Design that documents the why behind the what.

Personalisation at scale

Mass products and environments engineered to feel made-for-you. The design challenge of the next phase is the illusion, and the reality, of the personal inside the mass.

Designing for the senses

Sound, light, and smell moving from afterthought to the core of an experience. Once you have felt how a space should sound or be lit, you cannot unknow it.

Human scale over the bombastic

A turn toward developments that knit into existing urban fabric, like Diriyah Gate, over spectacle for its own sake. Provenance and human scale as markers of quality.

The end of import consulting

A shift away from generic, slide-deck-driven consulting toward authentic local identity and capability built from within. The era of imported best practice is closing.

A safe space to fail

The missing layer in a young design culture: room to sketch, experiment, take risks, and make mistakes. Iteration, more than the instant right answer, is how maturity arrives.

Roles in flux

New functions appearing fast, from accessibility specialists to chief experience and chief happiness officers. Job titles are morphing ahead of the institutions around them.

Saudi heritage as source

Craft, literature, poetry, and material traditions, from Jeddah woodwork to the mashrabiya, translated into contemporary graphic, spatial, and narrative design. A cultural priority and a commercial opportunity.

Gaming as a methodology

Less about designing games and more about gaming logic, interaction design, and systems thinking applied across architecture, education, and public space. Significantly underrepresented.

AI as process, not output

Using AI to sort data, generate questions, and stress-test thinking instead of producing finished designs. AI literacy is emerging as a rare, valuable skill in its own right.
