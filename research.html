Published research · 2026

Making a Design Culture

What allows a design culture to develop over time?

Based on a recorded dialogue with Harry Dobbs and Paulo Pimentel in May 2026, this paper examines who directs decisions, how original practice develops, and the place of experimentation, making, and public life.

The thematic analysis proposes five connected elements: agency, original practice, experimentation, material capacity, and public life over time. These provide a basis for further inquiry into how a design culture develops.

Lead Researcher and Author: Brandie Janow · Dialogue contributors: Harry Dobbs and Paulo Pimentel

Recorded reviews · 2025–2026

Learning from KoD programs

What should change between one gathering and the next?

Following the 2024 edition, two speaker feedback sessions in January and February 2025 examined overlapping sessions, navigation, seating, workshop structure, and follow-up. KoD’s subsequent review records changes made for the 2025 edition, including clearer signage and structured masterclasses.

A November 2025 event review identified difficulties with overlapping sessions, sound and seating, volunteer preparation, and follow-up. A September 2026 speaker discussion proposed more demonstrations and project examples, mentoring, and clearer responsibility for documentation.

The review distinguishes participant accounts, proposed responses, and changes recorded in later KoD documents. A recorded change does not, by itself, establish that it improved outcomes.

Educational inquiry

Design Education and Its Future

What should design students learn when artificial intelligence can generate images, text, code, and rapid proposals?

The original study was written independently for an advisory discussion at Iowa State University in 2024. It considered computational design alongside research, ethics, cultural understanding, STEAM, business skills, and industry relationships.

The continuing inquiry asks how students verify results, explain their decisions, and develop judgment when they use AI. A later advisory discussion raised differences in how individual professors approach these tools. That discussion does not establish a university-wide policy or an adopted curriculum.

Research by Brandie Janow · Inaugural member, Iowa State University Industrial Design Advisory Board (2024)

Design Ecosystems

What informs a design decision, and who lives with its results?

Choices about a logo, garment, interior, or building involve more than the designer. Funding, laws, local customs, suppliers, labor conditions, and the natural environment can all matter. The six areas below bring those relationships into one view.

Research by Brandie Janow · Architectural advisor: Nathan Saint Clare

Illustrative example

A public library

A library involves choices about site ecology, local languages and access, funding and regulations, the brief, construction, and long-term maintenance. The six areas help identify the people involved in each decision and the responsibilities that continue after opening.
